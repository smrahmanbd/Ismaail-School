Particles ↔ moles: the exchange counter

One rule does every conversion: the unit you have goes on the bottom, so it cancels.

The idea

A conversion factor is a fraction worth exactly 1 — the top and bottom are the same amount in different units, like 12 eggs1 dozen. Avogadro's number gives us two of them:

6.02 × 1023 particles1 mol  and  1 mol6.02 × 1023 particles

Multiplying by 1 never changes the amount — only the units it's written in.

Notebook Example Problem 1: convert 4.50 × 1024 atoms of zinc to moles.

  1. Analyze. Known: 4.50 × 1024 atoms Zn. Unknown: moles of Zn.
  2. Choose the factor. We have atoms, so atoms go on the bottom: 1 mol6.02 × 1023 atoms
  3. Cancel. 4.50 × 1024 atoms × 1 mol6.02 × 1023 atoms
  4. Divide the front numbers: 4.50 ÷ 6.02 = 0.7475. Subtract the powers: 1024 ÷ 1023 = 101.
  5. Answer: 0.7475 × 10 = 7.48 mol Zn. Check: a small number of moles ✔, three significant digits like 4.50 ✔.

Science-notation helper

Multiply: multiply the fronts, add the powers. Divide: divide the fronts, subtract the powers. On a calculator type 6.02 EXP 23 (or EE / ×10ˣ).
