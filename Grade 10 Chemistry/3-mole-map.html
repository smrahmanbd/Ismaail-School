Molar mass & the Mole Map

The periodic table secretly tells you how much one mole weighs. Moles are the bridge between grams and particles.

The idea

Molar mass = the mass of one mole = the number under the element's symbol on the periodic table, in g/mol. Carbon is 12.01, so 1 mol of carbon weighs 12.01 g.

You can never jump straight from grams to particles. You always cross the moles bridge.

    Trap: molar mass (g per mole) is not the mass of one atom. One carbon atom weighs 12.01 ÷ 6.02 × 1023 = 1.99 × 10−23 g.
