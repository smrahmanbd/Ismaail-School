Hydrates: the virtual lab

Some crystals have water built into them. Heat them, weigh the water that leaves, and work out the formula.

The idea

A hydrate is an ionic compound with a fixed number of water molecules in its crystal: CuSO₄·5H₂O is "copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate" — 5 waters per CuSO₄. Heating drives the water off, leaving the anhydrous salt.
