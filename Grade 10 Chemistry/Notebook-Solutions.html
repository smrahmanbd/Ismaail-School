The 4-step recipe for every calculation
  1. Analyze: write what you know (with units) and what you want.
  2. Plan: trace the path: MASS ⇄ MOLES ⇄ PARTICLES (moles are always in the middle).
  3. Solve: multiply by fractions so the unit you have is on the bottom and cancels.
  4. Check: right unit? sensible size? right number of significant digits?

Lesson 1 — Measuring Matter
VocabDefine: molecule, mole, Avogadro's number.
Think: Write what each word means.
  1. Molecule — two or more atoms joined together by covalent bonds (example: H₂O).
  2. Mole (mol) — the unit chemists use to count particles. 1 mole = 6.02 × 10²³ particles.
  3. Avogadro's number — the number 6.02 × 10²³, the number of particles in one mole.
ANSWERMolecule = bonded atoms · Mole = 6.02 × 10²³ particles · Avogadro's number = 6.02 × 10²³
ListList three common counting units and their values.
Think: Words that mean a fixed number of things.
  1. Think of things you buy in sets.
ANSWER1. pair = 2   2. dozen = 12   3. ream = 500 (also: gross = 144)
DescribeWhy did chemists need to invent a new counting unit?
Think: Why not just count atoms one by one?
  1. Atoms are incredibly small — a single drop of water has about 1.7 × 10²¹ molecules.
  2. Nobody can count that many, so we need one word that bundles a huge number together.
ANSWERAtoms are too small and too numerous to count individually, so chemists use the mole to count them in large, fixed bundles.
ListList three representative particles.
Think: What things can a mole count?
ANSWER1. atoms (e.g. Fe)   2. molecules (e.g. H₂O)   3. formula units (e.g. NaCl) — also ions (e.g. Na⁺)
AnalyzeThe usefulness of a conversion factor.
Think: Why do we multiply by fractions like 12 eggs / 1 dozen?
  1. A conversion factor has the same amount on the top and bottom, just in different units, so it equals 1.
  2. Multiplying by 1 changes the units but not the amount.
ANSWERIt lets you change units (e.g. particles → moles) without changing the actual quantity.
EquationEquation for number of particles in a number of moles; how to find moles from particles.
Think: Two directions of the same idea.
  1. Moles → particles: multiply by Avogadro's number.
  2. Particles → moles: divide by Avogadro's number (multiply by 1 mol / 6.02 × 10²³).
ANSWERparticles = moles × 6.02 × 10²³ particles1 mol    moles = particles × 1 mol6.02 × 10²³ particles
Get It?Two conversion factors from Avogadro's number.
Think: Write the fraction both ways up.
ANSWER6.02 × 10²³ particles1 mol  and  1 mol6.02 × 10²³ particles
Get It?Pennies in the photo: count one stack, then 12 stacks.
Think: Use 'pennies per stack' as the conversion factor.
  1. Count the pennies in one stack in your book's photo (call it P).
  2. Conversion factor: P pennies1 stack.
  3. 12 stacks × P pennies1 stack = 12 × P pennies.
  4. Example: if one stack has 20 pennies → 12 × 20 = 240 pennies.
ANSWERTotal pennies = 12 × (pennies in one stack). Use the number you count in your photo.
Example 1Convert 4.50 × 10²⁴ atoms of zinc to moles.
Think: How many 'bundles of 6.02 × 10²³' are there?
  1. Known: 4.50 × 10²⁴ atoms Zn; 1 mol Zn = 6.02 × 10²³ atoms. Unknown: mol Zn.
  2. We HAVE atoms → atoms on the bottom: 4.50 × 10²⁴ atoms × 1 mol6.02 × 10²³ atoms
  3. Divide the front numbers: 4.50 ÷ 6.02 = 0.7475. Subtract the powers: 24 − 23 = 1 → × 10¹.
  4. 0.7475 × 10 = 7.475 → round to 3 significant digits.
ANSWER7.48 mol Zn
✔ Check: The answer has 3 significant digits and is less than 10 mol (10 mol would be 6.02 × 10²⁴ atoms). ✔
Q7Explain why chemists use the mole.
ANSWERBecause atoms and molecules are extremely small and numerous; the mole lets chemists count them in convenient bundles by weighing.
Q8State the mathematical relationship between Avogadro's number and 1 mol.
ANSWER1 mol = 6.02 × 10²³ representative particles.
Q9List the conversion factors between particles and moles.
ANSWER6.02 × 10²³ particles1 mol and 1 mol6.02 × 10²³ particles
Q10Explain how a mole is similar to a dozen.
ANSWERBoth are counting units that stand for a fixed number: a dozen is always 12 things, a mole is always 6.02 × 10²³ things, no matter what the things are.
Q11How does a chemist count particles in a given number of moles?
ANSWERMultiply the number of moles by Avogadro's number: particles = moles × 6.02 × 10²³.
Q12Calculate the mass in amu of 0.25 mol of carbon-12 atoms.
Think: First find how many atoms, then each atom is 12 amu.
  1. Atoms = 0.25 mol × 6.02 × 10²³ atoms/mol = 1.505 × 10²³ atoms.
  2. Mass = 1.505 × 10²³ atoms × 12 amu/atom = 18.06 × 10²³ amu = 1.806 × 10²⁴ amu.
ANSWER1.8 × 10²⁴ amu
✔ Check: 0.25 has 2 significant digits, so 1.8 × 10²⁴.
Q13Representative particles in: a. 11.5 mol Ag   b. 18.0 mol H₂O   c. 0.150 mol NaCl   d. 1.35 × 10⁻² mol CH₄
Think: Moles → particles: multiply by 6.02 × 10²³ each time. Name the right particle!
  1. a. 11.5 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 69.23 × 10²³ = 6.92 × 10²⁴ atoms (silver is an element → atoms)
  2. b. 18.0 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 108.4 × 10²³ = 1.08 × 10²⁵ molecules (water is covalent → molecules)
  3. c. 0.150 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 0.903 × 10²³ = 9.03 × 10²² formula units (NaCl is ionic → formula units)
  4. d. 1.35 × 10⁻² × 6.02 × 10²³ = 8.127 × 10²¹ = 8.13 × 10²¹ molecules (powers: −2 + 23 = 21)
ANSWERa. 6.92 × 10²⁴ atoms · b. 1.08 × 10²⁵ molecules · c. 9.03 × 10²² formula units · d. 8.13 × 10²¹ molecules
Q14Arrange from smallest to largest number of particles: 1.25 × 10²⁵ atoms Zn, 3.56 mol Fe, 6.78 × 10²² molecules glucose.
Think: Put everything in the SAME unit (particles) before comparing.
  1. Fe: 3.56 mol × 6.02 × 10²³ = 21.4 × 10²³ = 2.14 × 10²⁴ atoms.
  2. Now compare the powers of ten: 10²² < 10²⁴ < 10²⁵.
ANSWERglucose (6.78 × 10²²) < Fe (2.14 × 10²⁴) < Zn (1.25 × 10²⁵)
Lesson 2 — Mass and the Mole
VocabDefine: conversion factor, molar mass.
ANSWERConversion factor — a ratio of equal values written in different units (equals 1). Molar mass — the mass in grams of one mole of a substance (unit: g/mol).
Fill-inAnalyze molar mass by completing the statements.
Think: Use the periodic table numbers, in grams.
ANSWERThe mass of one mole of carbon-12 atoms is 12 grams. The mass of one mole of hydrogen is 1.008 gram and is one-twelfth the mass of one mole of carbon-12. The mass of one mole of helium-4 is one-third the mass of one mole of carbon-12 and is equal to 4.00 grams. One mole of manganese is equal to 6.02 × 10²³ atoms of manganese.
MatchDraw a line from each type of conversion to its equation.
Think: The unit you START with must be on the bottom.
ANSWERmole → mass: moles × number of grams1 mol
mass → mole: mass × 1 molnumber of grams
mass → atoms: mass × 1 molnumber of grams, then × 6.02 × 10²³1 mol
atoms → mass: atoms × 1 mol6.02 × 10²³, then × number of grams1 mol
Get It?How are the jelly-bean conversion factors like molar mass?
ANSWERBoth link a COUNT to a MASS: 'grams per bag of jelly beans' is like 'grams per mole of atoms'. Each is a fixed mass for a fixed number of items.
You Try ItHow many atoms are in 10.0 g of pure copper (Cu)?
Think: Grams → moles → atoms (two steps; no shortcut).
  1. Known: mass = 10.0 g Cu. Periodic table: Cu = 63.55 → molar mass = 63.55 g/mol.
  2. Step 1 (g → mol): 10.0 g Cu × 1 mol Cu63.55 g Cu = 10.0 ÷ 63.55 = 0.1574 mol Cu
  3. Step 2 (mol → atoms): 0.1574 mol × 6.02 × 10²³ atoms1 mol = 0.9474 × 10²³ atoms
  4. Tidy: 0.9474 × 10²³ = 9.474 × 10²² → 3 significant digits.
ANSWER9.47 × 10²² atoms Cu
✔ Check: 10 g is about 1/6 of a mole (63.55 g), and 1/6 of 6.02 × 10²³ ≈ 1 × 10²³. ✔
Q22Why is understanding a system at the atomic scale important for relationships at a larger scale?
ANSWERWhat we measure in the lab (mass, how much reacts) comes from how atoms behave and combine. The mole connects the number of atoms to grams we can weigh, so atom-level rules (like formulas) let us predict lab-sized amounts.
Q23Conversion factor between mass and moles of fluorine.
Think: Look up F on the periodic table: 19.00.
ANSWER19.00 g F1 mol F  (or 1 mol F19.00 g F)
Q24How does molar mass relate the mass of an atom to the mass of a mole?
ANSWERIt's the same number with a different unit: one carbon atom is 12.01 amu, and one mole of carbon is 12.01 g.
Q25Steps to convert the mass of an element to the number of atoms.
  1. Multiply the mass by 1 molmolar mass (g) to get moles.
  2. Multiply the moles by 6.02 × 10²³ atoms1 mol to get atoms.
ANSWERg → mol (÷ molar mass) → atoms (× 6.02 × 10²³)
Q26Arrange from smallest to largest mass: 1.0 mol Ar, 3.0 × 10²⁴ atoms Ne, 20 g Kr.
Think: Convert everything to grams.
  1. Ar: 1.0 mol × 39.95 g/mol = 39.95 g (≈ 40 g)
  2. Ne: 3.0 × 10²⁴ ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 4.98 mol → 4.98 × 20.18 = 100.6 g (≈ 100 g)
  3. Kr: already 20 g
ANSWERKr (20 g) < Ar (≈ 40 g) < Ne (≈ 100 g)
Q27What is molar mass ÷ Avogadro's number? Show the units.
Think: Watch the units cancel.
  1. gmol ÷ atomsmol = gmol × molatoms = gatom
  2. Example: 12.01 g/mol ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ atoms/mol = 1.99 × 10⁻²³ g per atom.
ANSWERThe mass of ONE atom (in grams).
Q28Design a concept map for multistep conversions.
ANSWERMASS ⇄ MOLES ⇄ PARTICLES. Mass ↔ moles uses the molar mass; moles ↔ particles uses 6.02 × 10²³. You can't go from mass to particles without passing through moles.
Lesson 3 — Moles of Compounds
VocabDefine: representative particle.
ANSWERThe kind of particle a substance is made of: an atom, molecule, formula unit or ion.
DescribeRelationship between the mole information of a substance and its chemical formula.
ANSWERThe subscripts in the formula give the mole ratio: 1 mol of CCl₂F₂ contains 1 mol C, 2 mol Cl and 2 mol F.
Get It?How is a chemical formula used to find the number of moles in a given mass of compound?
ANSWERUse the formula to add up the molar mass (subscript × atomic mass for each element), then divide the given mass by that molar mass.
Example 6Moles of Al³⁺ ions in 1.25 mol Al₂O₃.

  1. Conversion factor: 2 mol Al³⁺1 mol Al₂O₃
  2. 1.25 mol Al₂O₃ × 2 mol Al³⁺1 mol Al₂O₃ = 2.50 mol Al³⁺
ANSWER2.50 mol Al³⁺
✔ Check: Twice the moles of compound — makes sense. ✔

StepsSteps to convert the mass of a compound to number of particles.
ANSWER1. Determine the molar mass. 2. Multiply by the inverse of the molar mass to convert to moles. 3. Multiply by Avogadro's number to calculate the number of formula units. 4. Use the ratios from the chemical formula to calculate the number of ions (or atoms). 5. Calculate the mass per formula unit (molar mass ÷ 6.02 × 10²³).
Q47How to find the molar mass of a compound; link to conservation of mass.
ANSWERAdd the molar masses of all its atoms/ions (subscript × atomic mass). Because mass is conserved, the compound's mass equals the total mass of the ions that make it — nothing is lost or gained when they combine.
Q48Conversion factors between moles and mass of a compound.

Q49How can you find the number of atoms or ions in a given mass of compound?
ANSWERgrams → moles (÷ molar mass) → formula units (× 6.02 × 10²³) → atoms or ions (× the subscript).

Lesson 4 — Empirical and Molecular Formulas
VocabDefine: percent by mass, percent composition, empirical formula, molecular formula.
ANSWERPercent by mass — (mass of element ÷ mass of compound) × 100. Percent composition — the percent by mass of every element in a compound. Empirical formula — the smallest whole-number ratio of atoms. Molecular formula — the actual number of each atom in one molecule (a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula).
EquationsEquations for percent by mass.
ANSWERFrom lab data: mass of elementmass of compound × 100    From a formula: mass of element in 1 mol of compoundmolar mass of compound × 100
Fill-inComplete the paragraph about empirical formulas.
ANSWER…empirical formula… smallest whole-number ratio… provides the subscripts… a whole-number multiple… percent composition… The mole ratios of elements must be rounded to whole numbers to be used as subscripts.
Get It?What data is needed to calculate percent composition of an unknown compound?
ANSWERThe total mass of the sample and the mass of each element in it (found by breaking the compound down and weighing the parts).
ExplainHow does a molecular formula tell apart two substances with the same empirical formula?
ANSWERThey have different molar masses, so the multiple n is different. Example: CH₂O (n = 1, formaldehyde) and C₆H₁₂O₆ (n = 6, glucose).
Example 12Empirical formula C₂H₃O₂, molar mass 118.1 g/mol → molecular formula.
Think: How many copies of the empirical formula fit into the molar mass?
  1. Empirical mass: C 2 × 12.01 = 24.02; H 3 × 1.008 = 3.024; O 2 × 16.00 = 32.00 → 59.04 g/mol
  2. n = 118.1 ÷ 59.04 = 2.00 → n = 2
  3. Multiply every subscript by 2.
ANSWERC₄H₆O₄
Flow chartSteps from percent composition to formulas.
ANSWERPercent → assume 100 g (grams) → ÷ molar mass (moles) → ÷ smallest (mole ratio) → if not whole, multiply by 2, 3, 4… → empirical formula → n = experimental molar mass ÷ empirical mass → multiply subscripts by n → molecular formula.
Q67A classmate says the molecular formula is 2.5 times the empirical formula. Correct?
Think: Can a molecule have half an atom?
ANSWERNo. n must be a whole number because molecules contain whole atoms. The data collected would be the mass (or percent) of each element and the compound's molar mass — something went wrong in the data or the calculation.
Q68174.86 g Fe and 75.14 g O. Empirical formula?
Think: Grams → moles → divide by the smallest → fix to whole numbers.
  1. Fe: 174.86 ÷ 55.85 = 3.131 mol
  2. O: 75.14 ÷ 16.00 = 4.696 mol
  3. Divide by smallest (3.131): Fe = 1, O = 1.50
  4. 1.50 isn't whole → multiply both by 2: Fe = 2, O = 3
ANSWERFe₂O₃

  2. O: 0.845 ÷ 16.00 = 0.0528 mol
  3. Ratio O ÷ Al = 2.61 — not close to a simple whole number or fraction. That's a warning sign!

Q70How are percent composition data related to element masses?
ANSWERIf you assume a 100 g sample, each percent equals that element's mass in grams (40.0 % C → 40.0 g C).
Q71How do you find the mole ratio in a compound?
ANSWERConvert each element's mass to moles, then divide every mole value by the smallest one.
Q72The molar mass is twice the empirical formula's mass. How are the formulas related?
ANSWERn = 2, so the molecular formula has every subscript doubled: molecular = (empirical)₂.
Q73Hematite (Fe₂O₃) or magnetite (Fe₃O₄): which gives more iron per kilogram?
Think: Find % iron in each.
  1. Fe₂O₃: molar mass = 2(55.85) + 3(16.00) = 159.70; % Fe = 111.70 ÷ 159.70 × 100 = 69.94 %
  2. Fe₃O₄: molar mass = 3(55.85) + 4(16.00) = 231.55; % Fe = 167.55 ÷ 231.55 × 100 = 72.36 %
ANSWERMagnetite (72.36 % Fe ≈ 724 g Fe per kg, vs 699 g for hematite)
Lesson 5 — Formulas of Hydrates
VocabDefine: crystal lattice, hydrate.
ANSWERCrystal lattice — the repeating 3-D arrangement of particles in an ionic solid. Hydrate — a compound with a specific number of water molecules bound in its crystal.
TablePrefixes for naming hydrates.
ANSWERmono- 1 · di- 2 · tri- 3 · tetra- 4 · penta- 5 · hexa- 6 · hepta- 7 · octa- 8 · nona- 9 · deca- 10
DescribeDescribe an anhydrate.
ANSWERThe compound left after all the water has been driven off a hydrate by heating ('an-' = without, 'hydrous' = water).
Get It?What data is needed to find a hydrate's formula, and how is it obtained?
ANSWERThe mass of the hydrate and the mass of the anhydrous solid after heating. Weigh the sample in a crucible, heat it until the mass stops changing, then weigh again.
You Try It5.00 g of barium chloride hydrate heats down to 4.26 g. Find the formula and name.
Think: Mass lost = water. Compare MOLES of water to MOLES of dry salt.
  1. Water lost = 5.00 − 4.26 = 0.74 g H₂O
  2. Moles of BaCl₂ = 4.26 g × 1 mol208.23 g = 0.02046 mol
  3. Moles of H₂O = 0.74 g × 1 mol18.02 g = 0.0411 mol
  4. x = 0.0411 ÷ 0.02046 = 2.01 ≈ 2
ANSWERBaCl₂·2H₂O — barium chloride dihydrate
Q76Summarize the composition of a hydrate.
ANSWERAn ionic compound plus a fixed number of water molecules per formula unit, held in its crystal lattice.
Q77Name SrCl₂·6H₂O and explain ·6H₂O.
Think: hexa = 6
ANSWERStrontium chloride hexahydrate. '·6H₂O' means six water molecules are attached to each formula unit of SrCl₂.
Q78Describe the procedure and calculations for finding the water in a hydrate.
ANSWER1. Weigh an empty crucible (so it can be subtracted). 2. Add hydrate and weigh again → mass of hydrate. 3. Heat to drive off water; cool and reweigh; repeat until the mass is constant (so all water is gone). 4. Water = hydrate − anhydrate. 5. Convert both to moles. 6. x = mol water ÷ mol anhydrate, rounded to a whole number.
Q790.050 mol H₂O for every 0.00998 mol of compound. Generalized formula?
Think: Divide.
  1. 0.050 ÷ 0.00998 = 5.01 ≈ 5
ANSWERX·5H₂O (a pentahydrate)
